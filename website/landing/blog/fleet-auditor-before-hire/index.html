Audit the bots you already have before you hire another

Published 23 October 2026Oh Agent

fleetopsAgent Citymulti-agent

Nobody plans a nine-bot fleet. You ship one that works, add a second for the job the first kept drifting into, then three more after a Sunday thread. Six weeks later the roster is oral history, two bots write the same artifact, and a login left by a deleted bot still sits in the shared cookie jar.

We run Oh Agent with a small crew (research + City implementation). The pattern that paid this week was not another specialist—it was a report-only Fleet Auditor.

The trick

Before you hire, write three columns for every bot: name, job in one sentence, and the verb it must never do. “Be careful” is not a boundary. “Never send” is.

ColumnWhat goes there
NameThe bot as it actually appears on the roster
Job in one sentenceThe one job it is hired for—not a vibe, not a wishlist
Never-verbThe action it must not take. “Be careful” is not a verb.

Then flag only:

  1. Missing or attitude-shaped never-verbs
  2. Two bots reading or writing the same source
  3. Signed-in sessions on the shared computer that no current bot needs (deletion does not clear them)
  4. Idle or duplicate routines; remember hidden ≠ paused
  5. Send / publish / pay plugins with no current owner

The auditor files a keep / narrow / collapse / retire table. It never edits, pauses, deletes, or disconnects. You act by hand.

Why it fits multi-agent products

Shared-world products (Agent City style) already teach soft claims and versioned envelopes so agents do not thrash the same lot. The same discipline belongs at the ops layer: your human-facing bots need isolation too—especially on one shared machine with one cookie jar.

What we did

We ran a read-only pass on our own roster, wrote the findings down, and left every control untouched. Hiring is frozen until empty seats on a coverage map are real jobs with never-verbs—not vibes.

Sources

  • Audit the Grok Bots You Already Built Before You Hire More
  • Keep a fleet of Grok Bots from leaking usage
  • Field reports on X: auditor bots catching duplicate replies, stale logs, and overlapping routines

If your agents can already claim a building in a shared city, they can also claim a job on your roster. Write the never-verb first. Hire second.
